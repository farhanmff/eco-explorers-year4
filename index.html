📑 Product design justification (Mayer + CASPER + ethics)

1. Visual design (CASPER)

Contrast: dark green text on light backgrounds, clear focus rings and answer states. Alignment: fixed card grids and a single visual hierarchy. Simplicity: one lesson section at a time to avoid competing tasks. Proximity: each explanation sits beside its activity. Emphasis: large titles, targets and next-step controls. Repetition: consistent buttons, colours and page headings. The pupil can move sequentially without teacher assistance.

2. Mayer's Cognitive Theory of Multimedia Learning

Coherence: decorative elements are limited around reading and quiz text so that unnecessary information does not compete with the learning task. Signalling: colour-coded 3D bins, target numbers and section labels highlight the important information. Spatial contiguity: bin descriptions appear next to the related visuals. Segmenting: the lesson separates the introduction, short video checkpoints, reading, sorting, assessment and reflection into manageable steps. Personalisation: learner-friendly directions and supportive chatbot language reduce complexity. Generative learning: learners categorise, explain, apply and reflect rather than only watch the video.

3. Critical evaluation and improvement plan

Strengths: the assessment gives immediate reasoned feedback; the 15-item sorting and 15-item reading check map to the specified objectives; the lower, middle and higher-tier prompts offer scaffolding. Limitations: Google Sites embeds can restrict video events and local browser storage; the video and external AI proxy depend on network availability; some pupils benefit from physical objects beyond the screen. Improvements: pilot with 5 pupils to observe accessibility and misconception patterns; use a secure teacher service for class-wide analytics; replace unsupported video pause points with a teacher-hosted interactive video; revise questions using response trends; offer Malay glossary support when needed.

4. Digital ethics, privacy and intellectual property

The external Little Fox video is credited to its creator and played through its official embedded player; it is not labelled as CC licensed. All interactive questions and code are original. The teacher portrait derives from the creator's supplied photograph. Learner identification is minimised through short codes; data are local by default. There is no secret key in browser code. Real pupil analytics and AI question processing require consent, authenticated access, clear retention rules, and an approved provider. Disclose any AI-generated visuals in final academic reporting.
